About

I start with a system that isn't working.

A city that keeps getting hotter. A neighbourhood without basic sanitation. A factory floor where one in seven castings fails inspection. I want to understand each one properly, then help fix it.

I grew up in Kolkata, a city that satellite records show has been getting measurably hotter for two decades. My first serious research used twenty years of NASA Landsat imagery to map that urban heat island. It was published in the International Journal of Science and Research and reached the semi-finals of the S.T. Yau High School Science Award.

Research made me want to build things. I founded ParticiPlan to survey 400+ people in an underserved neighbourhood and turn what they told us into costed infrastructure proposals, corporate funding and eco-friendly toilets. I then packaged the method into a web app so other NGOs can run it themselves. Most recently I spent a summer at Crescent Foundry bringing AI into quality control and production planning.

I'm now at UC Berkeley studying Physics and Economics, with a focus on deep tech, climate, policy and venture. I like problems that need both an equation and an argument.

Research · 01

Decoding Kolkata's urban heat island

Published · International Journal of Science and Research · Jan 2026

A two-decade (2005–2024) geospatial and remote-sensing analysis of how heat has spread through one of India's densest megacities, and what it would take to cool it again.

PythonNASA LandsatGoogle Earth EngineLSTNDVINDBICorrelation Analysis

Mentored by Prof. Vivek Agarwal (Northumbria University). In Python on Google Earth Engine, I derived land-surface-temperature and vegetation indices from NASA Landsat imagery. I built seasonal snapshots for January, April, July and October across twenty years to map heat and vegetation loss neighbourhood by neighbourhood. The paper was a semi-finalist for the S.T. Yau High School Science Award, and I've since selected 50+ cities to extend the model to.

01 · DATAMulti-temporal Landsat thermal + optical imagery
02 · INDICESLST, NDVI and NDBI per seasonal composite
03 · STATSTrend detection and LST–NDVI correlation
04 · POLICYHotspot-targeted mitigation aligned to SDG 11 & 13
  1. Heat is spreading outward. High-temperature zones that were once confined to industrial, transport and commercial cores now cover large parts of the peri-urban fringe.
  2. April peaks approach ~51 °C in the densest areas, including Burrabazar, Tangra and Salt Lake's commercial sectors. Several fringes now exceed 50 °C.
  3. The city is cooling down less after the monsoon. October once brought relief. Now inner-city wards often stay at or above 44 °C, a sign of growing thermal inertia.
  4. Green cover is the counterweight. Across the study, vegetation and surface temperature are strongly inversely correlated, which supports urban afforestation, riverfront greening and reflective-surface mandates.

Founder · Aug 2024 – Present

ParticiPlan: infrastructure designed with the people who use it.

A participatory urban-remediation project in Kolkata. We surveyed the community, ranked solutions by how well they would work rather than how easy they were to deliver, and found the money to build them. The idea is simple and rarely tested: infrastructure works better and lasts longer when the people who live with it help design it.

Now it's software. A web app packages the survey-to-proposal method so any community can run it on its own.
